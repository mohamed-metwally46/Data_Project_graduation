## SM-02 joint feasibility design and implementation

The baseline witness uses disjoint four-product blocks with observed Category A anchors. The [selection methodology](PRODUCT_MARKET_SELECTION.md), [allocation proof](SYNTHETIC_JOINT_ALLOCATION_FEASIBILITY.md), and [SM-03 handoff](SM03_COMPANY_PLANNING_HANDOFF.md) document the implemented approach. The steps below also describe broader constraints for later scenarios.

1. Resolve an explicit business-location allowlist from approved geography and provenance. Filter observed supplier and importer product roles independently. Exclude unapproved statistical and historical locations, and apply concentration caps by role.
2. Build role adjacency from the pinned observed data and permitted bilateral edges from the market engine. Mark optional B edges and keep C outside the default planner. Screen mapped product candidates for active listing, buyer, and competitive response feasibility before diversity selection.
3. Allocate product and market coverage together. Use deterministic category quotas and tie-breaking. Track 300 products, 300 supplier slots and 200 importer slots, with one initial role per company. Multiple company slots may share a geography if policy permits. Give selected products both roles and active listing coverage under the final coverage rules.
4. Plan 1,000 distinct supplier-product listing slots from the portfolios. Reserve active positions by product and RFQ needs before assigning other statuses. Check role capacities, stock and MOQ feasibility against approved unit and range fields later.
5. Allocate 600 RFQ scenario slots and 900 offer slots jointly. A targeted request can have zero or one response from its target. Open requests with two or three responses need distinct suppliers with eligible active listings for that product and reachable importer. An RFQ with a request beyond a listing's capacity must remain unanswered or be handled by a separately approved policy.
6. Keep the denominator for bilateral policy explicit. Listings and open RFQs without a supplier stay outside it. Include targeted RFQs and offers when both need bilateral evidence, according to the approved final policy. Reserve A capacity before drawing B. Reconcile category totals after integer rounding and scenario assignment.
7. Start with count bounds and stable greedy allocation with bounded deterministic repair. Use bipartite capacity matching for bottlenecks such as product coverage or eligible RFQ respondents, and backtrack on blocked category or geography constraints. Record unsatisfied constraints and rerun with approved assumptions before considering an optimizer. The SM-02 proof is a validated plan with capacity and policy witnesses before any company identities or commercial records are produced.

## Preliminary checks and limits

At the proposed averages, 300 suppliers give roughly 1,200 portfolio slots and 200 importers roughly 600. One listing per supplier-product association means 1,000 listings require at least 1,000 distinct supplier associations, using about 83% of those proposed slots. With 850 proposed active listings, covering 300 products requires at least 300 active listings before reserving repeated supplier positions for competition. The proposed response distribution fits the RFQ and offer totals and needs at least 300 open requests for competing replies.

These are arithmetic checks. They cannot prove that diverse product quotas, eligible geography, active listing capacity, distinct supplier slots, bilateral caps and exact scenario totals can all be met together. The [readiness report](../reports/synthetic_marketplace_readiness.json) contains measured source breadth indicators before the business-location filter. Counts of distinct exporter markets are geographic breadth indicators. Several distinct suppliers could share a market, and the extract establishes no company capacity. No 300-product set or company allocation was selected in SM-01.

Before the business-location filter, 4,577 mapped products show both observed roles. Of those, 4,211 have at least one importer market reached directly by three or more distinct exporter markets for the product. This is a breadth screen for future planning. It does not guarantee three eligible active suppliers after applying geography, portfolios, stock and bilateral rules.
